
# Squaring the Generator
## Introduction
A finite field $F_q$ (a field with $q$ elements) and its nonzero elements $F^*_q$ form a cyclic multiplicative group. Within this group, a generator is an element that can generate all the nonzero elements through successive powers. The multiplicative group $F^*_{q}$ has $q-1$ elements and is cyclic, meaning there exists a generator $\omega$ such that:
$$
F^*_{q} = \{\omega^1, \omega^2,\dots, \omega^{q-2}\} = \langle\omega\rangle
$$
where $\langle\omega\rangle$ denotes the cyclic subgroup generated by $\omega$ (the set of all integer powers of $\omega$).

In the Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) over finite fields for polynomial multiplication, leveraging the cyclic group structure is essential. By computing powers of the generator g (particularly squaring), we can:

1. Effectively halve the computational domain
2. Focus on specific subgroups (like the $n$-th roots of unity)

This transformation provides significant computational advantages for FFT operations.

## Squaring the Generator in Groups of Power of two Order

Consider $F_{7} = \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\}$. The multiplicative group $F^*_{7}$ has $q-1=7 - 1= 6$ elements (excluding $0$), and it is cyclic, meaning has a generator $\omega$.

A valid primitive 6th root of unity is $\omega = 3$, because $3^{6} \equiv 1\pmod{7}$ and no lower power (positive integer) of $3$ gives $1$. Thus:
$$
\langle 3 \rangle = \{1, 2, \dots, 6\} = F^*_{7}
$$

The following Python code utilizes the `galois` library to identify primitive elements (generators) of the multiplicative group in $F_7$:

```python
import galois

GF = galois.GF(7)

primitive_elements = GF.primitive_elements
print("Primitive elements:", primitive_elements)
# Primitive elements: [3 5]

# Alternatively, find a single primitive element
# suitable when there are a lot of primitive elements
primitive_element = GF.primitive_element
print("A primitive element:", primitive_element)
# A primitive element: 3
```

### Squaring $\omega$
When we square the generator $\omega = 3$, we obtain a new generator $\omega^2\equiv 2\pmod{7}$ (since $\omega^2 = 3^2 = 9 \equiv 2\pmod{7}$). This element $2$ generates the cyclic subgroup:
$$
\langle 2 \rangle = \{2^k|k\in\mathbb{Z}\}= \{1, 2, 4\}
$$
where all powers satisfy:
$$
2^0 = 1, 2^1 = 2, 2^2 = 4, 2^3 \equiv 1
$$

This subgroup $\langle 2\rangle = \{1 , 2, 4\}$ constitutes the 3rd roots of unity in $F^*_{7}$, with $2$ being a primitive 3rd root of unity.

Note that squaring the original generator $\omega = 3$ reduces the domain from the full group $\{1, 2, \dots, 6\}$ (order 6) to this subgroup of order 3.

At this point, continued squaring doesn’t produce anything new, and ultimately, the subgroup collapses to $\{1\}$.

### Remark
For the purpose of multiplying polynomials with coefficients in $F_q$ and use in FFT, we are interested in integers $n$ of form $n = 2^k$.

The multiplicative group $F^*_q$ is a finite cyclic group of order $q-1$. Therefore, there exist a generator $g$ such that $g$ it generates $F^*_q$.

Suppose $n$ divides $q-1$. Then there exist a primitive $n$-th root of unity $\omega = g^{\frac{q-1}{n}}$, and the cyclic subgroup $\langle\omega\rangle$ of order $n$ consists exactly of all $n$-th roots of unity in $\mathbb{F}_q$
$$
\langle\omega \rangle = \{1, \omega^1, \omega^2, \dots, \omega^{n-1}\}
$$
The subgroup generated by $\omega^2$ ($\langle\omega^2\rangle$) has order equal to half of the order of $\langle\omega \rangle$.
$$
\mathrm{ord}(\langle\omega^2\rangle) = \frac{\mathrm{ord}(\langle\omega\rangle)}{2}
$$
This is because the squaring operation effectively skips every other element in the cycle generated by $\omega$.
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle\omega^2 \rangle &= \big\{(\omega^2)^0, (\omega^2)^1, (\omega^2)^2, \dots, (\omega^2)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\big\}\\
&= \big\{1, \omega^2, \omega^4, \dots, \omega^{n-2}\big\}    
\end{aligned}
$$
Repeat the process until $n=1$. Continue halving the subgroup size by squaring the generator, halving $n$, and recalculating the roots of unity.

### Example 2
Consider the finite field $F_{17} = \{0, 1, 2, \dots, 16\}$. Its multiplicative group $F^*_{17}$ consisting $q-1 = 17-1 = 16$ elements (excluding $0$), forms a cyclic group with generator $\omega$.

#### Step 1
A valid primitive 16th root of unity is $\omega = 3$, because $3^{16} = 1$ and no lower power(positive integer) of $3$ gives $1$.
$$
\langle 3 \rangle = \{3^0=1,3^1, 3^2,\dots, 3^{15}\}
$$
Explicitly, 
$$
\langle 3 \rangle =\{1 , 3, 9, 10, 13, 5, 15, 11, 16, 14, 8, 7, 4, 12, 2, 6\}\space(\text{all 16 elements})
$$
The cyclic subgroup $\langle 3 \rangle = F^*_{17}$ consists of all 16th roots of unity in $F_{17}$, with $\omega =3$ being a primitive 16th root of unity.

#### Step 2
When we square the generator $\omega = 3$, we obtain a new generator $\omega^2\equiv 9\pmod{17}$ (since $\omega^2 = 3^2 = 9\pmod{17}$). The element $9$ generates the 8th roots of unity cyclic subgroup:
$$
\langle 9 \rangle = \{9^k|k\in\mathbb{Z}\}= \{1, 9, 13, 15, 16, 8, 4, 2\}
$$
where all powers satisfy:
$$
9^0 = 1, 9^1 = 9, 9^2 \equiv 13, 9^3 \equiv 15, 9^4 \equiv 16, 9^5 \equiv 8, 9^6 \equiv 4, 9^7 \equiv 2
$$
The subgroup $\langle 9 \rangle$ consists of the 8th roots of unity in $F_{17}$, forming a cyclic group of order 8 where $9$ acting as a primitive 8th root of unity.

Note that squaring the original generator $\omega = 3$ reduces the domain from the full group $\{1 , 3, 9, 10, 13, 5, 15, 11, 16, 14, 8, 7, 4, 12, 2, 6\}$ (order 16) to this subgroup $\{1, 9, 13, 15, 16, 8, 4, 2\}$ of order 8.

#### Step 3
Squaring the new generator (a primitive 8th root of unity) $9$ yields:
$$9^2 = 81 \equiv 13$$
where $13$ is now a primitive 4th root of unity. The cyclic subgroup generated by $13$ consists of all 4th roots of unity:
$$
\langle 13 \rangle = \{13^k|k\in\mathbb{Z}\} = \{1, 13, 16, 4\}
$$
with the explicit calculations:
$$
13^0 = 1, 13^1 = 13, 13^2 \equiv 16, 13^3 \equiv 4, 13^4 \equiv 1
$$
Note that in this step, squaring the previous step generator $9$ reduces the domain from the 8th roots of unity subgroup $\{1, 9, 13, 15, 16, 8, 4, 2\}$ (order 8) to the 4th roots of unity subgroup $\{1, 13, 16, 4\}$ of order 4.

#### Step 4
Starting with the primitive 4th root of unity $13$, squaring yields:
$$
13^2 \equiv 16
$$
where 16 is a primitive 2nd root of unity. The cyclic subgroup generated by 16 consists of the 2nd roots of unity:
$$
\langle 16 \rangle = \{16^k|k\in\mathbb{Z}\} = \{16^0, 16^1\} = \{1, 16\}
$$


Note that squaring the previous generator $13$ reduces the domain from the order 4 subgroup $\langle 13 \rangle = \{1, 13, 16, 4\}$ to the order 2 subgroup $\langle 16 \rangle = \{1, 16\}$

### Remark
Note that in the finite field $F_{17}$, we observe the following congruence relations
$$
\begin{aligned}
&16 \equiv -1\pmod{17}\quad\text{or}\quad -16 \equiv 1\pmod{17}\\
&13 \equiv -4\pmod{17}\quad\text{or}\quad -13 \equiv 4\pmod{17}\\
&15 \equiv -2\pmod{17}\quad\text{or}\quad -15 \equiv 2\pmod{17}\\
&8 \equiv -9\pmod{17}\quad\text{or}\quad -8 \equiv 9\pmod{17}\\
\end{aligned}
$$
This leads to the following subgroup reformulations:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\langle 16 \rangle = \{1, 16\} = \{1, -1\} = \{\pm 1\}\\
&\langle 13 \rangle = \{1, 13, 16, 4\} = \{1, -4, -1, 4\} = \{\pm 1, \pm 4\}\\
&\langle 9 \rangle = \{1, 9, 13, 15, 16, 8, 4, 2\} = \{1, -8, -4, -2, -1, 8, 4, 2\} = \{\pm 1, \pm 2, \pm 4, \pm 8\}
\end{aligned}
$$

The following Python code performs successive halving of the group size by squaring the generator, showing how higher-order roots decompose into smaller subgroups:

```python
# Define the prime and its generator
p = 17  # Prime field: F_p
g = 3   # Generator of the multiplicative group F_p*

# Function to compute all n-th roots of unity in F_p
def compute_roots_of_unity(p, g, n):
    roots = [(g**k) % p for k in range(n)]  # Calculate powers of g modulo p
    return roots

# Function to perform halving steps
def halving_steps(p, g, n):
    current_roots = compute_roots_of_unity(p, g, n)  # Full group (n roots)
    print(f"n={n} roots of unity: {current_roots}")
    
    step = 1
    while n > 1:
        g = (g**2) % p   # Square the generator
        n = n // 2       # Halve the subgroup size
        current_roots = compute_roots_of_unity(p, g, n)
        print(f"Step {step}: n={n} roots of unity: {current_roots}")
        step += 1

# Prime Field F_17: Roots of Unity example
n = 16  # Choose a power of 2
halving_steps(p, g, n)
```
The code provides a clear computational demonstration of how roots of unity form nested subgroups in finite fields.
```python
// Outputs

step 2: n=8 roots of unity: [1, 9, 13, 15, 16, 8, 12, 6]
Step 3: n=4 roots of unity: [1, 13, 16, 12]
Step 4: n=2 roots of unity: [1, 16]
```

# Summary
**Field $F_q$ and generator of $F^*_q$:**

The multiplicative group of nonzero elements, $F^*_q$, is cyclic and can be generated by a primitive element (generator) $\omega$.

**Squaring the Generator:**

Squaring $\omega$ (i.e., $\omega^2$) yields a new generator, which skips every alternate element in the group.

This halves the group size, reducing the domain of $n$-th roots of unity.

**Iterative Halving:**

Repeatedly squaring the generator continues halving the group size, eventually collapsing it to $\{1\}$.

Practical applications include evaluating polynomials over finite fields by successively reducing the computational workload.
